01
01 — THE PROBLEM

How do you know the new build is safe?

Today the answer is 30 to 100 real-robot trials — expensive, slow, and never quite the same twice. Or it is a simulation job in CI that goes red often enough that someone re-runs it until it is green.

Neither is evidence. Both are how a regression reaches a live floor.

02
02 — METHOD · STEPS 1, 2 AND 4 BUILT · CI INTEGRATION IN ACTIVE IMPLEMENTATION

Four steps. Three are built.

STEP 01

Bring your platform

URDF-to-USD import, plus assisted MJCF and runtime integration. We do this with you — it is not push-button today.

STEP 02

Encode behaviours

Each becomes a scenario spec with an explicit pass/fail condition, living in your repo.

STEP 03

Gate the merge

The GitHub Action runs today against a certified baked scenario. Running your behaviours on every pull request is in active implementation, not shipped.

STEP 04

Read the reason

A failed verdict comes back reason-coded against a recorded baseline — not just red.

04
04 — FIT · A NARROW FIT, ON PURPOSE

Who this is for.

This is for teams running an active ROS 2 autonomy stack on real robots — not simulation-only — who have one regression that actually hurt: an incident or near-miss the team still talks about.

Everything else — platform files, scope, how much of your engineers’ time this needs — we work out on the call. If it is not a fit we say so there, early and plainly, rather than finding out slowly together.

Ground robotics — warehouse and AMR — is the primary domain. Aerial autonomy is adjacent, and we would say so on the call rather than on this page.
